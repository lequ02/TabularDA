PAGE 7
LMER models
• While previous linear models (LM) helped explore interactions and main effects, they treat all factors as 
fixed. However, our data has a structure where observations might not be fully independent. Specifically, F1 
scores are likely correlated within the same dataset because different methods are applied to it. 
• Furthermore, we might consider y_synth (the synthesizer for the target variable) and tvae (whether TVAE 
was used for feature synthesis) as sources of random variation, representing sample from a larger 
population of possible datasets or synthesis configurations.
• Goals: 
1. We want to see if the new>old (for approach), with dataset, tvae,&y_synthas a random effect. 
Meaning, the new approach is better for potentially anydataset, any X_synthesizer, and any 
y_synthesizer. 
2. We want to see if including y when training the X_synthesizer is better than not including y. I.e, 
has_y=1 > has_y=0

PAGE 11
 Contrast between old vs. new Aprroach 
• The contrast performed on the lm model with 1 interaction effect shows that: 
• The new approach is statistically better than the old approach by 7.36% across 6 datasets, 2 X 
synthesizers, regardless of whether y was included in training the X_synthesizer 
• The LMER model shows that the contrast of newApproach_vs_oldApproach = 0.07516 when (dataset & 
tvae) are considered random effects. 
• These 2 models are consistent with each other and reaffirms the conclusion that the new approach is 
better than the old approach.

Conclusions
1. The new approach is better than the old approach by 0.075 point of F1-score macro. This 
is a relative increase of 11% 
2. Across 6 datasets, 6 y synthesizers, regardless of X_synthesizer (ctgan or tvae), including 
y in the X_input increases the result only marginally (0.3%). However, this effect is not 
statistically significant (p_value=0.5091>0.05).
