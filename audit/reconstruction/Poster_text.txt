Introduction
Generating high-fidelity synthetic tabular data
is challenging due to mixed data types
(discrete/continuous) and complex column
distributions (multimodal, imbalanced). While
generative models like GANs (e.g., CTGAN, TVAE)
have advanced the field, a gap often remains
between synthetic and real data quality. This work
aims to improve the realism of generated
structured data.
References
1. Breiman, Leo. "Random forests." Machine learning
45 (2001): 5-32.
2. Chen, Tianqi, and Carlos Guestrin. "Xgboost: A
scalable tree boosting system." Proceedings of the
22nd acm sigkdd international conference on
knowledge discovery and data mining. 2016.
3. Goodfellow, Ian, et al. "Generative adversarial
networks." Communications of the ACM 63.11
(2020): 139-144.
4. Xu, Lei, et al. "Modeling tabular data using
conditional gan." Advances in neural information
processing systems 32 (2019).
Conclusion
 Generating Xsyn & ysyn separately will
result in synthetic data that more closely
resemble distribution and properties of
the real data, demonstrated through the
higher F1-macro scores
 Don’t include y in X_input to save
computational power and time
Results
On average, the new approach (ours) is statistically better
(p_value <.0001) than the old approach by 7.36% across 6
datasets, 2 X synthesizers, regardless of whether y was included in
training the X_synthesizer.
Across 6 datasets, 6 y synthesizers, regardless of
X_synthesizer (ctgan or tvae), including y in the X_input increases
the result only marginally (0.3%). However, this effect is not
statistically significant (p_value=0.5091>0.05).

features, etc.)
Data & Evaluation
We evaluate synthetic data quality using its
practical utility in a ML task across 6 datasets.
1. A Deep Neural Network (DNN) is trained only
on the generated synthetic data.
2. The DNN is then tested on unseen real data (the
test set).
3. We measure this performance using the F1-
macro score, where higher scores (max 1)
indicate better accuracy.
Strong performance by the DNN on real data
signifies that the synthetic data realistically
captures the original data's patterns.

We propose a decoupled/composite synthesis pipeline, separating feature (Xsyn) and target (ysyn) generation, to replace the
current modulus pipeline outlined in Xu et al. [4].
Additionally, we investigate the impact of excluding the target y during the training of the feature generator (Xsynthesizer​) on

Introduction
Generating high-fidelity synthetic tabular data is
challenging due to complex column distributions
(multimodal, imbalanced) and mixed data types
(discrete/continuous). While generative models like
GANs (e.g., CTGAN, TVAE) have advanced the field, a
gap often remains between synthetic and real data
quality. This work aims to improve the realism of
generated synthetic structured data.
References
1. Breiman, Leo. "Random forests." Machine learning
45 (2001): 5-32.
2. Chen, Tianqi, and Carlos Guestrin. "Xgboost: A
scalable tree boosting system." Proceedings of the
22nd acm sigkdd international conference on
knowledge discovery and data mining. 2016.
3. Goodfellow, Ian, et al. "Generative adversarial

(2020): 139-144.
4. Xu, Lei, et al. "Modeling tabular data using

processing systems 32 (2019).
Conclusion
 Decoupled Synthesis Enhances Utility:
Generating features (Xsyn) and targets
(ysyn) separately produces higher-quality
synthetic data compared to end-to-end
methods, evidenced by significantly
improved downstream F1-macro scores.
 Optimize Feature Generation: Excluding
the target variable (y) during feature
synthesis is recommended; it offers
computational savings with negligible
impact on final model performance.
Results
Our decoupled synthesis approach significantly improves upon
standard end-to-end generation:
• Achieved an average F1-macro improvement of 0.0736 (10.86%
relative increase) over baseline. This significant gain was
consistent across 6 datasets and various generative models
• Including target y during feature X synthesis offered negligible
benefit, yielding only a marginal (+0.3% F1-macro) and
statistically insignificant (p=0.5091) improvement. This indicates
potential for model simplification without performance loss
Future Work
More Validation: Evaluate the proposed
pipeline across a more extensive and diverse
range of datasets.
Task Expansion: Adapt and test the
decoupled approach for regression tasks, not
just classification.
Xsynthesizer Research: Implement advanced
techniques (e.g., incorporating Fourier
features) to further improve the quality of the
feature synthesizer.

Strong performance by the DNN on real data
signifies that the synthetic data realistically captures
the original data's patterns.
